## Slide 2: The Problem

**Headline:** {{Describe the problem in the prospect's language}}

**Key Points:**
- {{Pain point 1 with data — e.g., "Teams waste 15 hours/week on manual X"}}
- {{Pain point 2 with data — e.g., "60% of companies report Y as their top challenge"}}
- {{Pain point 3 with emotional resonance — e.g., "Engineers hate doing Z instead of building"}}

**Visual:** {{Chart showing the problem's magnitude, or a relatable illustration}}

> **Speaker Notes:** This is the most important slide. The prospect should be nodding along — if they don't see themselves in this problem, the rest of the deck won't land. Ask: "Does this resonate with your experience?" Pause for their answer. Use their response to tailor the rest of the conversation. Cite the data source for credibility.

## Slide 3: The Solution

**Headline:** {{product_name}}: {{What it does in one sentence}}

**Key Points:**
- {{Benefit 1 — tied directly to pain point 1}}
- {{Benefit 2 — tied directly to pain point 2}}
- {{Benefit 3 — tied directly to pain point 3}}

**Visual:** {{High-level product screenshot or conceptual diagram showing the solution}}

> **Speaker Notes:** Bridge from the problem to the solution naturally: "What if you could [solve problem] without [current painful approach]?" Keep it high-level here — don't dive into features yet. Focus on outcomes, not mechanics. The goal is to create a "tell me more" reaction.

## Slide 4: How It Works

**Headline:** Simple to start, powerful at scale

**Flow:**
1. {{Step 1 — e.g., "Connect your existing tools in 2 minutes"}}
2. {{Step 2 — e.g., "AI analyzes your workflow and suggests optimizations"}}
3. {{Step 3 — e.g., "Automate the repetitive work, focus on what matters"}}

**Visual:** {{Product walkthrough — 3-4 screenshots showing the key flow, or a short embedded demo video}}

> **Speaker Notes:** Walk through a real use case, ideally one relevant to the prospect. If doing a live demo, keep it under 3 minutes and follow the happy path. Have a backup recording in case of technical issues. Emphasize ease of setup — time-to-value is a major selling point. Ask: "Would you like to see how this would work with your specific [data/workflow/use case]?"

## Slide 11: Roadmap

**Headline:** Where we're going

| Timeline | Milestone | Description |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Shipped** | {{feature/milestone}} | {{brief description}} |
| **This Quarter** | {{feature/milestone}} | {{brief description}} |
| **Next Quarter** | {{feature/milestone}} | {{brief description}} |
| **H2 {{year}}** | {{feature/milestone}} | {{brief description}} |
| **Future** | {{vision item}} | {{brief description}} |

> **Speaker Notes:** The roadmap should show velocity and vision. Don't commit to specific dates — use quarters. If the prospect has a feature request, check if it's on the roadmap and, if so, highlight it. If not, say: "That's great feedback — let me bring that to our product team." Never promise something that isn't planned. The roadmap should build confidence that the product will continue to improve.

## Slide 12: Ask / Call to Action

**Headline:** Ready to {{achieve the outcome}}?

**Next Steps:**
1. {{CTA 1 — e.g., "Start your 14-day free trial today"}}
2. {{CTA 2 — e.g., "Schedule a personalized demo with your data"}}
3. {{CTA 3 — e.g., "Get a custom ROI analysis for your team"}}

**Contact:**
- {{name}} — {{email}} — {{phone}}
- {{website_url}}
- {{calendar_booking_link}}

> **Speaker Notes:** End with a clear, single ask. Avoid giving too many options — recommend the one that makes most sense for this prospect. If the meeting went well, push for a specific next step with a date: "Can we schedule a pilot kickoff for next Tuesday?" If they need to involve others, offer to present to the broader team. Always follow up within 24 hours with a summary email, the deck, and any materials discussed. Leave them with a reason to act now, not later.
